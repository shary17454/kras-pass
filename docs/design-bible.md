# DAWWAMA — design bible

*دوّامة — الدليل التصميمي*

---

## The pitch

Four competitors are pulled into **the Dawwama**, a vortex that sits at the
meeting point of eight realms. Whatever falls in is judged by contest: a rapid
sequence of short, unfair, entirely winnable games. Nobody dies. Everybody
loses, repeatedly, in front of their friends.

The feeling to hit is the one from a crowded room and a single screen: rounds
short enough that losing does not sting, rules simple enough to explain while
the countdown runs, and enough depth that the person who has played most still
usually wins.

## Design pillars

1. **Explainable in one sentence.** If the rules card needs a third sentence,
   the game is too complicated.
2. **Readable at a glance.** Four bodies, four colours, four distinct
   silhouettes, one camera. A player must be able to find themselves in half a
   second with everything moving.
3. **Controlled chaos, not randomness.** Power-ups swing rounds; physics does
   not. Balls travel in straight lines and bounce mirror-true. Knockback is
   deterministic. If you lost, you can say why.
4. **Short rounds, high replay.** 75–120 seconds. The best outcome of any round
   is someone saying "again".
5. **Losing is funny.** Falling off has a big sound, a big spin and a fast
   return. Elimination is never a punishment screen.

## The world

The Dawwama is a slow vortex of stone and light. Eight realms feed into it, and
each sends one competitor to the Grand Cup. The realms are places, not factions:
there is no lore to read and no villain. The tournament is the story.

| Realm | Feel | Arena palette |
|---|---|---|
| Green Vale / وادي الخضرة | Overgrown terraces, the first ring most competitors ever fall out of | greens, warm light |
| Tide Hollow / غور المدّ | Half-submerged shelves, water that keeps deciding the round | blues, teal accents |
| Ember Souq / سوق الجمر | Market floors above a forge, heat shimmer, hanging lamps | oranges, deep reds |
| Sky Loft / علّية السماء | Platforms that are not attached to anything | violets, pale sky |
| Cogworks / دار التروس | The machine that turns the vortex | steel, amber warning lights |
| Stone Reach, Dune Sea, Storm Rim | Home realms of three competitors; not yet venues | — |

## The roster

Eight competitors, one stat budget. Every character spends the same 3.00 points
across speed, acceleration, weight, jump, power and control — so the roster is a
set of trade-offs, never a ladder. This is asserted by the test suite, not by
good intentions.

| | Realm | Identity | The trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nabta** نبتة | Green Vale | A sprout that grows through cracks | Quick and nimble, but light — the easiest character to shove |
| **Sakhra** صخرة | Stone Reach | A boulder with a temper | Nearly immovable and hits hardest; arrives late to everything |
| **Fanoos** فانوس | Ember Souq | A lantern with a steady flame | Perfectly average — the reference character and the honest pick |
| **Ramla** رملة | Dune Sea | A drift of sand that will not settle | Explosive acceleration, poor control at speed |
| **Barq** برق | Storm Rim | A spark that arrives before its sound | Fastest and lightest; one clean hit ends you |
| **Mowja** موجة | Tide Hollow | A wave that bends and returns | Best turning and air control, unremarkable everywhere else |
| **Ghaim** غيم | Sky Loft | A cloud with opinions | Enormous jump and glide; weak on the ground |
| **Turs** ترس | Cogworks | A gear built to outlast the round | Heavy bruiser, second only to Sakhra, and slow with it |

Unlocks: Nabta, Sakhra, Fanoos and Ramla are available immediately. Barq at 6
trophies, Mowja at 14, Ghaim after 15 wins, Turs after 2 tournaments. The
adventure introduces every competitor as an opponent long before you can play
them, so the roster reads as a cast rather than a shop.

## Visual language

Original throughout, and deliberately built from primitives.

- **Toon shading, high chroma, hard silhouettes.** Diffuse and specular toon
  ramps with a rim light. No textures anywhere.
- **One accent colour per arena**, used for the rim, the hazards and the danger
  cues, so the thing that will kill you is always the brightest thing on screen.
- **A ground ring under every competitor** in their colour. This is the single
  most valuable readability element in a four-player game and it is never
  switched off.
- **Squash and stretch** on landing, hitting, and being hit. Primitives feel
  alive when they deform.
- **Shard bursts instead of particle clouds.** Ten solid shards read against a
  busy arena; a soft particle cloud does not.

Everything is generated by `MeshFactory` at runtime, so re-skinning the game is
editing numbers, and swapping in authored models later means implementing one
builder branch per shape.

## Audio language

Also generated, by `Synth`, at runtime.

- **Music** is a four-bar loop per context: bass root, a sine pad, an arpeggio
  and a kick/hat pattern. Six styles — menu, two arena flavours, tension,
  victory, adventure — differing only in tempo, scale degrees and waveform.
- **Effects** are short, square/triangle/noise, with visible pitch sweeps.
  Retro-adjacent because that is what reads over four people shouting.
- **Every impact has a sound and a screen shake proportional to its force.**
  Force is the same number that drives the knockback, so audio, camera and
  physics never disagree about how hard something hit.

## Balance intentions

- **Nobody is out for long.** Games that eliminate resolve in under 90 seconds;
  games that do not eliminate respawn in 1.4 seconds.
- **The leader is a target.** Gem Grab spills gems when you are hit; Zone Hold
  scores only for an uncontested occupant; higher-skill AI actively goes after
  whoever is winning.
- **Power-ups swing, they do not decide.** The strongest single effect is a
  1.85× knockback multiplier for seven seconds. Nothing grants invulnerability
  for longer than a shield's one hit.
- **Sudden death is fast and brutal.** The ring collapses, the water doubles,
  the arms speed up. Overtime never lasts.

## What this is not

It is not a graphics showcase, it is not a story game, and it is not a
simulation. It is twenty-one small rule-sets on one very solid engine, tuned so
that the eleventh round of the evening is still worth playing.
